**Proposition 2.1.** *Let $E=\bigoplus_{i=1}^nE_i$ be a direct sum. Then we have an isomorphism*

$$
F\otimes E\leftrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^n(F\otimes E_i).
$$

*Proof.* The isomorphism is given by abstract nonsense. We keep $F$ fixed, and consider the functor $\tau:X\mapsto F\otimes X$. As we saw above, $\tau$ is linear. We have projections $\pi_i:E\to E$ of $E$ on $E_i$. Then

$$
\pi_i\circ\pi_i=\pi_i,\quad\pi_i\circ\pi_j=0\quad\text{if }i\neq j,
$$

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n\pi_i=\mathrm{id}.
$$

We apply the functor $\tau$, and see that $\tau(\pi_i)$ satisfies the same relations, hence gives a direct sum decomposition of $\tau(E)=F\otimes E$. Note that $\tau(\pi_i)=\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi_i$.

**Corollary 2.2.** *Let $I$ be an indexing set, and $E=\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_i$. Then we have an isomorphism*

$$
\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_i\right)\otimes F\approx\bigoplus_{i\in I}(E_i\otimes F).
$$

*Proof.* Let $S$ be a finite subset of $I$. We have a sequence of maps

$$
\left(\bigoplus_{i\in S}E_i\right)\times F\to\bigoplus_{i\in S}(E_i\otimes F)\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}(E_i\otimes F)
$$

the first of which is bilinear, and the second is linear, induced by the inclusion of $S$ in $I$. The first is the obvious map. If $S\subset S'$, then a trivial commutative diagram shows that the restriction of the map

$$
\left(\bigoplus_{i\in S'}E_i\right)\times F\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}(E_i\otimes F)
$$

induces our preceding map on the sum for $i\in S$. But we have an *injection*

$$
\left(\bigoplus_{i\in S}E_i\right)\times F\to\left(\bigoplus_{i\in S'}E_i\right)\times F.
$$

Hence by compatibility, we can define a bilinear map

$$
\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_i\right)\times F\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}(E_i\otimes F),
$$

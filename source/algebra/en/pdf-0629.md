whence a commutative diagram

![Commutative diagram for a finite flat module over a local ring](/library/volume-86/diagrams/pdf-0629-local-flat-module.png)

in which the rows are exact. Since $M$ is assumed flat, the map $h$ is an injection. By the snake lemma one gets an exact sequence

$$
0\to\operatorname{coker}f\to\operatorname{coker}g\to\operatorname{coker}h,
$$

and the arrow on the right is merely

$$
R^{(n)}/\mathfrak mR^{(n)}\to M/\mathfrak mM,
$$

which is an isomorphism by the assumption on $x_1,\ldots,x_n$. It follows that $\operatorname{coker}f=0$, whence $\mathfrak mN=N$, whence $N=0$ by Nakayama if $R$ is Noetherian, so $N$ is finitely generated. If $R$ is not assumed Noetherian, then one has to add a slight argument as follows in case $M$ is finitely presented.

**Lemma 3.9.** *Assume that $M$ is finitely presented, and let*

$$
0\to N\to E\to M\to 0
$$

*be exact, with $E$ finite free. Then $N$ is finitely generated.*

*Proof.* Let

$$
L_1\to L_2\to M\to 0
$$

be a finite presentation of $M$, that is an exact sequence with $L_1,L_2$ finite free. Using the freeness, there exists a commutative diagram

![Commutative diagram comparing a finite presentation with the given exact sequence](/library/volume-86/diagrams/pdf-0629-finite-presentation.png)

such that $L_2\to E$ is surjective. Then the snake lemma gives at once the exact sequence

$$
0\to\operatorname{coker}(L_1\to N)\to 0,
$$

so $\operatorname{coker}(L_1\to N)=0$, whence $N$ is an image of $L_1$ and is therefore finitely generated, thereby proving the lemma, and also completing the proof of Theorem 3.8 when $M$ is finitely presented.

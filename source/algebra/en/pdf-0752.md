*In particular for $F=F_0$,*

$$
F(E')F(E'')\subset F(E).
$$

**Proof.** We may assume $E'$ is a submodule of $E$. We pick generators $x_1,\ldots,x_p$ of $E'$ and elements $y_1,\ldots,y_q$ in $E$ such that their images $y''_1,\ldots,y''_q$ in $E''$ generate $E''$. Then $(x,y)$ is a family of generators for $E$. Suppose first that $m\le p$ and $n\le q$. Let $A$ be a matrix of relations among $y''_1,\ldots,y''_q$ with $q$ columns. If $(a_1,\ldots,a_q)$ is such a relation, then

$$
a_1y_1+\cdots+a_qy_q\in E'
$$

so there exist elements $b_1,\ldots,b_p\in R$ such that

$$
\sum a_iy_i+\sum b_jx_j=0.
$$

Thus we can find a matrix $B$ with $p$ columns and the same number of rows as $A$ such that $(B,A)$ is a matrix of relations of $(x,y)$. Let $C$ be a matrix of relations of $(x_1,\ldots,x_p)$. Then

$$
\begin{pmatrix}B&A\\C&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

is a matrix of relations of $(x,y)$. If $D''$ is a $(q-n)\times(q-n)$ subdeterminant of $A$ and $D'$ is a $(p-m)\times(p-m)$ subdeterminant of $C$ then $D''D'$ is a

$$
(p+q-m-n)\times(p+q-m-n)
$$

subdeterminant of the matrix

$$
\begin{pmatrix}B&A\\C&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

and $D''D'\in F_{m+n}(E)$. Since $F_m(E')$ is generated by determinants like $D'$ and $F_n(E'')$ is generated by determinants like $D''$, this proves the proposition in the present case.

If $m>p$ and $n>q$ then $F_{m+n}(E)=F_m(E')=F_n(E'')=R$ so the proposition is trivial in this case.

Say $m\le p$ and $n>q$. Then $F_n(E'')=R=F_q(E'')$ and hence

$$
F_m(E')F_n(E'')=F_q(E'')F_m(E')\subset F_{p+n}(E)\subset F_{m+n}(E)
$$

where the inclusion follows from the first case. A similar argument proves the remaining case with $m>p$ and $n\le q$. This concludes the proof.

**Proposition 2.8.** *Let $E',E''$ be finite $R$-modules. For any integer $n\ge0$ we have*

$$
F_n(E'\oplus E'')=\sum_{r+s=n}F_r(E')F_s(E'').
$$

## §3. UNIVERSAL DERIVATIONS AND THE DE RHAM COMPLEX

*In this section, all rings $R,A$, etc. are assumed commutative.*

Let $A$ be an $R$-algebra and $M$ an $A$-module. By a **derivation** $D:A\to M$ (over $R$) we mean an $R$-linear map satisfying the usual rules

$$
D(ab)=aDb+bDa.
$$

Note that $D(1)=2D(1)$ so $D(1)=0$, whence $D(R)=0$. Such derivations form an $A$-module $\operatorname{Der}_R(A,M)$ in a natural way, where $aD$ is defined by $(aD)(b)=aDb$.

By a **universal derivation** for $A$ over $R$, we mean an $A$-module $\Omega$, and a derivation

$$
d:A\to\Omega
$$

such that, given a derivation $D:A\to M$ there exists a unique $A$-homomorphism $f:\Omega\to M$ making the following diagram commutative:

![Universal derivation diagram](/library/volume-86/diagrams/pdf-0754-universal-derivation.png)

It is immediate from the definition that a universal derivation $(d,\Omega)$ is uniquely determined up to a unique isomorphism. By definition, we have a functorial isomorphism

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Der}_R(A,M)\approx\operatorname{Hom}_A(\Omega,M)}.
$$

We shall now prove the existence of a universal derivation.

The following general remark will be useful. Let

$$
f_1,f_2:A\to B
$$

be two homomorphisms of $R$-algebras, and let $J$ be an ideal in $B$ such that $J^2=0$. Assume that $f_1\equiv f_2\bmod J$; this means that $f_1(x)\equiv f_2(x)\bmod J$ for all $x$ in $A$. Then

$$
D=f_2-f_1
$$

is a derivation. This fact is immediately verified as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_2(ab)&=f_2(a)f_2(b)=[f_1(a)+D(a)][f_1(b)+D(b)]\\
&=f_1(ab)+f_1(b)D(a)+f_1(a)D(b).
\end{aligned}
$$
